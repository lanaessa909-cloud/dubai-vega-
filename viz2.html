2. Encoding

The visualization has three linked views.

  • View 1 – map. Grey shapes are the Dubai Municipality communities. Each circle is a metro station, placed at its longitude and latitude. Size = number of unit and villa sales in communities within 3 km of the station. Colour = median sale price per m² of those sales (dark purple = cheaper, yellow = more expensive). Stations with no sales within 3 km are not drawn. The selected station gets a thick outline. The busiest stations have a name label (DMCC is shown as DMCC / JLT, as the station was later renamed).
  • View 2 – line chart. x-axis = year, y-axis = median price per m² (AED). Each thin grey line is one station. The selected station is drawn as a thick red line with points. Years with fewer than 20 sales for a station are left out, because a median of a handful of sales jumps around (e.g. Nakheel 2025 was based on 18 sales).
  • View 3 – bar chart. x-axis = distance from the community to its nearest metro station (under 1 km, 1–2 km, 2–5 km, 5–10 km, over 10 km), y-axis = median price per m², colour = property type (unit = blue, villa = orange). The distance band of the selected station is highlighted (outline, other bands faded).

3. Interaction

  • Hover a station: name, line, number of sales within 3 km and median price per m².
  • Click a station: the line chart highlights that station's price over time (a point selection on station drives a filter in View 2), and the bar chart highlights the distance band of that station's sales (a second selection on dist_bin).
  • Double-click: clears the selection.
  • Hover a bar, a grey line (station name) or a point in the red line for exact values and the number of sales.

4. Value added

  • A buyer or investor can see which metro stations have the most sales and which are the most expensive per m², and how each station's price changed since 2015.
  • The bar chart tests the common belief that "closer to the metro means pricier". It does not hold simply: homes within 1 km of a station are cheaper than those 1–2 km away, because about 70% of the sales within 1 km are around DMCC / Jumeirah Lakes Towers and the southern Route 2020 station R72, areas cheaper than downtown Dubai. The map shows why: location matters more than the metro itself.
  • City planners can see which stations sit in busy residential markets (large circles) and which have almost no sales around them (24 of 54 stations have none within 3 km).

Why not use nearest_metro_en? About 32% of rows are empty, some names are renamed stations (Jumeirah Lakes Towers = DMCC, First Abu Dhabi Bank = FGB, ADCB = Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank), and others are not metro stations at all (Palm Jumeirah, Marina Towers, Harbour Tower). Matching on text would give wrong stations, so we assign stations by geography. Rows with and without a listed station have almost the same median price (about 15,600 vs 15,300 AED/m²).

Limits: distance is measured from the community centre, not from the building, so it is approximate and the same for every sale in a community. The station list is from about 2020, so stations opened or renamed since are missing or have old names. The Foursquare venue data was not used in this chart.
