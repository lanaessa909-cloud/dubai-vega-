Dubai Real Estate Transactions – Vega-Lite (Part 2)

Three visualizations of Dubai Land Department transactions, made with Vega-Lite. Every chart only uses transactions from 1 January 2015 to 31 August 2026.

Task 1 – Transactions by day of the week

This chart counts all transactions. The weekday is worked out in UTC from the registration date, so it shows the same result on any computer.

Loading chart…
Question: How many transactions were registered on Fridays? 6,553 transactions were registered on Fridays. That is much lower than Tuesday to Thursday (roughly 13,200 to 13,700 each) and Monday (20,126). Saturday and Sunday have none.

What the chart shows: nothing is registered on the weekend, and Friday is low. Almost all of the Friday transactions are from 2022 onwards, because the UAE changed its weekend from Friday and Saturday to Saturday and Sunday in January 2022 (299 Friday transactions before 2022 and 6,254 after). Monday is the busiest day. One possible reason is that deals from the weekend get registered on the first working day, but the data cannot prove that.

Question: In which month were off-plan sales highest, and how many?September 2025 had the highest number of off-plan sales in the supplied dataset, with 706 sales.

What the chart shows: from 2015 to 2020 off-plan and existing sales moved at similar, fairly flat levels, with a sharp dip in April–May 2020 during COVID-19 (existing sales fell to 19 in April 2020). From 2021 both grew strongly; existing sales led in 2021–2022, but from 2023 off-plan sales pulled ahead and roughly doubled existing sales by 2025, reflecting the developer-led boom of projects sold before completion. Both lines are lower in 2026 than at the 2025 peak. Hover over a line for the exact monthly count.

Task 3 – Median price per m² of units and villas by community

Sales of units and villas only, 1 Jan 2015 – 31 Aug 2026. Each Dubai Municipality community is coloured by the median price per square metre of its sales. Hover over a community to see its name, number of sales and median price.

No unit/villa salesFewer than 10 sales (median not reliable)

Colour choices. Prices are strongly right-skewed: most communities sit between about 8,000 and 20,000 AED/m², while a few luxury communities are far above. A linear scale would make almost every community the same light colour, so the map uses a quantile scale (5 classes, each holding about the same number of communities) with a sequential orange–red palette. A median of only a handful of sales can be extreme by chance, so communities with fewer than 10 sales are shown in grey instead of a price colour. Communities with no unit/villa sales stay visible in light grey, so all communities appear on the map. The Mercator projection is used because it keeps shapes correct at city scale.

Question: Which community has the largest number of sales of units and villas, and how many sales is it? Is this visualization effective in finding it? Why? Checking the source data…
No, the map is not effective for this question. Colour encodes the median price; the number of sales is not encoded at all and is only available in the tooltip, so a reader has to hover over every community one by one to find the busiest one. The size of each shape also shows the community's land area, not how many sales it has, so large desert communities catch the eye even when they have few sales. A sorted bar chart of sales per community, or a symbol map with circle size = number of sales, would answer this question at a glance. The map is effective for its own purpose: showing where prices are high (the coast, Palm Jumeirah, Downtown and Business Bay) and where they are low (inland and outer communities).
